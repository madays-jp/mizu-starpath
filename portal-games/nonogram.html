How to play

Just 3 things to remember

It's simple: look at the numbers at the edges and work out which squares to fill. No math needed. Once every square is decided, the picture is complete.

  1. 1
    A number means "fill that many in a row"

    The numbers on the left and top are your hints. If a row's number is "3", somewhere in that row there are 3 squares in a row to fill.

    5= fill all 5 squares
    3= fill 3 in a row (the column numbers decide where)
  2. 2
    Two numbers mean a gap in between

    "1 1" means fill 1 square, leave at least 1 square empty, then fill 1 more. The numbers always come in order, left to right (or top to bottom).

    1 1×= leave at least 1 square between them
    2 1×= 2 + a gap + 1
  3. 3
    Combine rows and columns to pin down each spot

    A row alone may not settle things, but add the column numbers and you'll find squares that can only go one way. For squares you know stay empty, use "Mark" mode to put a × on them so you don't lose track.

Mini example — a little cross
131 1 3 1

The middle row says "3", so fill all of it. The middle column also says "3", so fill all of it too. That tells you the four corners stay empty — and the cross is complete. That's the basic idea.

First tip: start with Easy 5×5 below. Rows and columns with big numbers (like "5" or "4") are the easiest places to spot fills.
